Manuscript Figure 2, interactiveAnatomy of an effective prompt

The quality of what comes out of an LLM is strongly influenced by how the task is framed — prompt structure measurably affects the quality and validity of generated content. A well-formed prompt has five components. Toggle any of them off below and watch what the model is left to assume. Manuscript · Figure 2

Omitting a component leads the model to fall back on generic assumptions — a default reader, a default length, no flag on the claims you most need to check.

Build the prompt

Act as an experienced hematopathology attending teaching a PGY-2.
I am a PGY-2 in my first month of blood bank, two days of
didactics in.
Explain MGUS in three layers.
Three paragraphs, layer name at the start of each, 3–5 sentences.
Flag any numerical threshold to verify against current IMWG
criteria.

Then: iterate — draft → observe → refine

The first draft is rarely the keeper; refine until it holds up, then verify. Prompts improve through iteration rather than in a single pass. Two habits from the manuscript's governance layer belong in the prompt itself:

  • Name the version. Many tasks ask a model to apply an external rubric — a classification, a protocol, a milestone framework — and those rubrics are revised on their own schedule. A prompt that encodes a superseded standard produces fluent output measured against the wrong yardstick, and nothing in the response signals it.
  • Ask for uncertainty. A verification cue does not make the model honest; it makes the places to check visible. Verification itself still falls to you.

Prompting deserves explicit teaching as a practical AI-literacy skill — it is a competency the learner carries into every pillar of the framework. For a teachable one-hour version, see the guide How to write a good prompt.
