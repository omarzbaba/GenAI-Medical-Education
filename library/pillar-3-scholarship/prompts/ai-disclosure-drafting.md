## What this prompt does

Drafts the AI-use disclosure statement your target journal requires.
Editorial governance is explicit: ICMJE and COPE state that AI cannot be
an author because it cannot take responsibility for the work, while human
authors remain accountable for AI-assisted content. Publisher policies
increasingly require disclosure, though wording and placement vary.

## When to use it

At submission time, once you know exactly what AI touched. The disclosure
should identify the tool and its role in the manner required by the
target journal.

## The prompt

```
Draft an AI-use disclosure statement for a manuscript where a large
language model was used only for language editing of the Introduction
and Discussion under author supervision.
```

Adjust the described role to match what actually happened — the statement
is only as honest as its inputs.

## Expected output

A short declaration in the style journals expect, e.g.: "During the
preparation of this work the authors used [tool] to improve the
readability and language of the [sections]. After using this tool, the
authors reviewed and edited the content as needed and take full
responsibility for the content of the publication."

## Common failure modes

- Generic wording that does not match the target journal's required
  declaration format or placement.
- Understates the role AI actually played, if your description does.

## Required human verification

Check the statement against the target journal's and ICMJE requirements —
AI is not an author, humans remain accountable, and the wording must
match the journal's declaration format. The factual content of the
disclosure is an integrity obligation, not boilerplate.

## Best model and why

Workhorse tier — this is formatting a known genre; the honesty of the
description is supplied by you.
